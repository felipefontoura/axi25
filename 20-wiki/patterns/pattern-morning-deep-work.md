# [EXEMPLO] Morning Deep Work Produces Disproportionate Learning Gains

## Observation

On days where the first 60-90 minutes of the morning are used for focused study or knowledge-system work — before email, before Slack, before social media — both the quality of new notes and the subjective sense of progress are significantly higher than on days where the morning is reactive.

The pattern is not just about time quantity. 90 minutes of morning deep work produces more usable output than 3 hours of fragmented afternoon sessions. The mechanism appears to be decision fatigue and attentional depletion: by midday, the cognitive resource available for difficult linking and synthesis is already spent.

## Evidence

- 2025-12-18: Spent 90 minutes before 9am working through Ahrens' book; wrote four notes. Afternoon session (2h) produced none.
- 2026-01-02: New year, deliberately protected 7–9am for study. First week showed 3× normal note output.
- 2026-01-08: Broke the pattern (morning meeting). Note output that day: zero.
- 2026-01-15: Full vault setup session completed entirely in the 7–9am block. Most productive single session recorded → [[learning]]

## Implications

- The [[learning]] area is most sensitive to schedule design, not available hours
- Morning blocks should be treated as non-negotiable commitments, same as external meetings
- This pattern suggests that protecting the morning is a higher-leverage decision than any particular study technique

## Suggested Actions

- Block 7–9am daily as "learning time" in the calendar with no exceptions for non-urgent meetings
- Move all reactive work (email, Slack, admin) to after 10am
- Track the pattern with a weekly signal review to see if the confidence rating should rise to `high`
