# [EXEMPLO] Spaced Repetition

## Idea

Spaced repetition schedules reviews of material at increasing intervals, exploiting the spacing effect to maximise long-term retention for a given amount of review time.

## Detail

The spacing effect has been replicated more consistently than almost any finding in cognitive psychology: distributing practice over time produces far more durable memory than the same total time spent in a single massed session. Spaced repetition algorithms (Leitner, SM-2, FSRS) formalise this into a review schedule that adapts to your actual forgetting curve.

The practical implication is counter-intuitive: you should review material just as you are about to forget it, not while it still feels fresh. That slight difficulty of retrieval — the "desirable difficulty" in Robert Bjork's terminology — is what drives encoding depth. Reviews that feel too easy are not helping much.

In a note-taking system, spaced repetition works best when the units being reviewed are genuinely atomic. A flashcard that asks "what is Chapter 4 about?" cannot be answered consistently and trains no durable schema. A card that asks "what is the one-sentence claim of the spacing effect?" can be, and each correct retrieval strengthens a precise memory trace.

The tool matters less than the habit. Anki, Obsidian-spaced-repetition, paper Leitner boxes — all work if used consistently. The scheduling algorithm is a rounding error compared to whether you actually sit down every morning and do the reviews.
