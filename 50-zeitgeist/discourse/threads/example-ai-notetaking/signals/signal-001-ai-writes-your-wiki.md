# [EXEMPLO] Karpathy: "Your LLM should be writing your wiki"

## Excerpt

> "your LLM should be writing your wiki, not just answering your questions"
> "stop using AI as a search engine you talk to. start using it as a librarian who lives in your notes and maintains them for you"

(From the Jan 12 thread — 18K likes, 4K reposts within 48h)

## Zeitgeist signal

This reframe is entering the public conversation at exactly the moment when "AI for productivity" discourse has stalled at the chatbot-as-assistant level. The "librarian not search engine" phrase is the kind of one-liner that spreads because it does two things at once: it demotes the dominant mental model (Q&A tool) and installs a new one (active curator) in the same sentence.

The thread is performing well with the dev-community audience proxy but the idea is not yet in the mainstream productivity discourse (Notion users, corporate PKM tools, etc.). That gap is the signal: there is a 6-12 month window where this framing is still "ahead of the curve" for the mainstream audience.

## Why it lands

Hot-take mechanics: declarative ("should be"), implies the listener is doing it wrong, offers a better path in the same breath. The "librarian" metaphor is concrete and transferable — it works for technical and non-technical audiences. Karpathy's credibility amplifies reach.

The thread reply from @simonw adds useful tension: "schema discipline is a tax most people won't pay." That friction point is real and will be the counter-narrative when this idea goes mainstream.
